# Suspended Light (떠 있는 빛)

**Philosophy:** what the eye sees in a quiet room is not the air but what the air is carrying, and only where light happens to cross it.

A room at dawn holds countless fine particles, and almost all of them stay invisible. They show up only where a beam passes through and catches them for a moment. This movement takes that as its rule. The particles exist everywhere in the field, uniformly and patiently, but their visibility is a function of light. The algorithm does not draw dust; it draws the intersection of dust and illumination. A meticulously crafted visibility function, the product of deep computational expertise, decides for every mote how much of it the viewer is allowed to see.

The light source sits just outside the frame, high and to one side, the way a low sun enters through a window. From it, a small family of soft wedges fans across the canvas. Each wedge is seeded: its angle, width, and strength are drawn once from the random stream and never change for that seed. Brightness decays with distance from the source and with distance from the wedge's center line, so the edges of every beam dissolve rather than cut. Calibrating that falloff took painstaking iteration; the goal is a beam you notice only after a second look.

Motion, where it exists, is slow and almost reluctant. A low-frequency noise field leans gently in one prevailing direction, downward and away from the light, as settling particles do. The noise adds just enough lateral wander that no two paths are parallel, while the dominant direction keeps the composition calm. Paths are short. Nothing races, nothing swirls. Every velocity in the system was chosen by a master-level hand to suggest time passing without asking for attention.

Density is the third voice. Particles accumulate where the field is slow and thin out where it moves, so sifted, uneven clusters form naturally without being placed. A rare few carry a toasted, warmer tint, a quiet hint that this air has been somewhere near heat. Color is strictly limited to a warm ground, a paler cream for light, one golden glow at the source, and a sparing reddish warmth. Contrast stays deliberately low, because this field is meant to sit beneath words and must never compete with them.

Every seed yields a different morning: a different set of beams, a different scatter, a different drift. The same seed always returns the same morning exactly. The result should read as the product of painstaking optimization and countless refinements, a living system whose beauty is in how little it insists on itself.
