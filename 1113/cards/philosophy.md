# Oven Ledger (오븐 장부)

**Philosophy:** time kept by hand, on paper, in a working room.

A single sheet clipped to a warm wooden board is the whole world of this movement. Every composition is that sheet: a rectangle of pale paper, held by a clip at its upper edge and set a fraction of a degree off true, the way a sheet sits when someone in a hurry has just pinned it down. The board around it is never decoration. It is the margin, the breathing room, the proof that the page lives somewhere real. Space is generous and deliberate, and the paper always gets more of it than the words do.

Color comes from the material, not from a palette. Warm beige is the board, cream is the paper, chocolate brown is the printed ink, and terracotta is the hand: every mark a person adds with a grease pencil. One saturated yellow is allowed, and only once per sheet. It means "this one, now." Because it is rationed, its single appearance carries the whole hierarchy. Each placement is meticulously crafted, calibrated until the one bright note sits exactly where the eye should land and nowhere else.

Two voices write on every sheet and they never trade roles. The printed voice is a sturdy, even sans-serif. It states facts: times, names, prices. The handwritten voice is quick and slightly tilted. It does the human work: ticks, circles, a short warm line. The contrast between them is the typography. Scale does the rest: one element is very large, everything else is quiet. That ratio is the product of painstaking attention, tested over and over until it feels inevitable.

Structure is borrowed from the ledger: hairline dashed rules, a column of times, tick marks laid out in patient rows. Repetition is the pattern language. Twenty-four small strokes can be a day, three rows a list, an empty dashed box a promise to be filled later. Nothing is numbered unless it really is a sequence, and nothing is boxed unless it really holds something. Every alignment reads as master-level execution: the left edges agree, the baselines agree, and the margins agree across the whole set.

A series in this movement reads as pages torn from the same pad on the same morning. The clip, the margins, the footer, and the two voices repeat exactly. Only the content and the small tilt of each sheet change, so the set holds together the way a stack of real paper does. The finished work should look as if someone at the top of their field spent countless hours on a few simple sheets, until nothing could be removed and nothing was missing.
